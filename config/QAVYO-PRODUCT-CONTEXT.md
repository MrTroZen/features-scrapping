# Qavyo Product Context

## 1. What Qavyo Is

Qavyo is a connected restaurant operating platform.

Its goal is to help restaurant operators run the major parts of their business from one connected system instead of relying on many disconnected software products.

Qavyo is not intended to be only:

- a POS
- an ordering platform
- an inventory tool
- an HR tool
- an analytics dashboard

The long-term product direction is a connected restaurant operating system where operational data from different areas can work together.

The product should help restaurants:

- run daily operations
- understand business performance
- reduce unnecessary manual work
- identify operational problems
- make better decisions
- automate repetitive processes where appropriate
- connect data that normally lives in separate systems

---

## 2. Initial Commercial Market

Qavyo's initial commercial focus is the United Kingdom.

Primary users include:

- independent restaurants
- small restaurant groups
- growing multi-location restaurant businesses

Useful research from other markets, including the United States, should still be collected.

However, geography-sensitive information must be clearly identified.

Examples include:

- taxes
- labour law
- minimum wages
- tipping
- payment fees
- delivery economics
- employment practices
- regulations
- accounting rules
- consumer behaviour

Do not assume US-specific benchmarks automatically apply to UK restaurants.

## 3. Core Product Philosophy

Qavyo should generally follow these principles.

### Connected

Restaurant systems should share data where useful.

Examples:

POS sales can affect:

- inventory
- menu analytics
- forecasting
- labour analysis
- financial reporting
- AI recommendations

Inventory can affect:

- food-cost calculations
- purchasing
- menu profitability
- waste detection
- recipe costing

Staff data can affect:

- labour percentage
- scheduling
- productivity
- forecasting

The value of Qavyo should increase when its modules work together.

---

### Operationally Useful

Features should solve real restaurant problems.

Avoid building features solely because:

- competitors have them
- AI can technically generate them
- they sound impressive
- they produce more dashboards

Prefer features that help a restaurant:

- understand something important
- make a decision
- take action
- prevent a problem
- save time
- improve profitability
- improve operational control

---

### Simple

Qavyo should make complex restaurant operations easier to understand.

Do not create unnecessary complexity for restaurant owners and managers.

Where possible:

data → insight → recommended action

is better than:

data → more data → more charts → user figures everything out manually

---

### Action-Oriented Intelligence

Analytics should not stop at displaying numbers.

Where the available data justifies it, Qavyo should help users understand:

- what happened
- why it may have happened
- whether it matters
- what should be investigated
- what action could be taken

Example:

Weak:

"Food cost: 34.7%"

Better:

"Food cost increased from 30.8% to 34.7%."

More useful:

"Food cost increased from 30.8% to 34.7%.
Most of the increase came from chicken and cooking oil.
Purchase prices increased while menu prices remained unchanged."

Possible action layer:

"Review supplier pricing and the profitability of affected menu items."

The system must not invent causes when the available data cannot support them.

## 7. What Research Is Usually Low Priority

Unless it contains meaningful operational knowledge, deprioritise:

- motivational business advice
- personal branding
- influencer promotion
- sponsorships
- generic entrepreneurship
- vague growth advice
- social-media hacks
- generic advertising advice
- creator lifestyle content
- promotional claims about the creator's own product

Do not discard useful operational insight simply because it appears inside marketing content.

Extract the useful part and ignore the rest.

## 8. Product Opportunity Standard

Restaurant knowledge should not automatically turn into a Qavyo feature.

A strong potential Qavyo capability usually has several of these properties:

1. Solves a meaningful restaurant problem.
2. Can use data Qavyo realistically has or could reasonably capture.
3. Produces a useful decision or action.
4. Saves time or reduces manual work.
5. Improves operational visibility.
6. Can reduce cost, waste or risk.
7. Can improve revenue or margin.
8. Connects information across Qavyo modules.
9. Is repeatable for many restaurants.
10. Is understandable to operators.

---

## 9. Weak Feature Patterns

Be sceptical of ideas that are mainly:

- another dashboard
- another generic AI chatbot
- another arbitrary score
- an AI summary with no action
- an alert for something that is not actionable
- a feature based on unavailable data
- an extremely niche workflow
- duplication of existing information
- an impressive-looking metric with little operational value

---

## 10. Data-First Feature Thinking

When evaluating a potential feature, think in this order:

Restaurant problem

↓

Required data

↓

Does Qavyo have or reasonably capture the data?

↓

Calculation / logic / model

↓

Insight

↓

Decision

↓

Action

↓

Business outcome

Do not start with:

"Let's add AI."

AI is a method, not the objective.

## 11. Intelligence Example

Suppose Qavyo observes:

- chicken purchase cost increased 14%
- chicken-based menu prices did not change
- sales volume remained similar
- contribution margin declined

A useful intelligence system may identify:

Observed:
Supplier cost increased.

Calculated:
Item-level contribution margin declined.

Possible consequence:
Profitability of several menu items has fallen.

Recommended investigation:
Review supplier pricing and menu pricing.

This is stronger than:

"AI detected something interesting about chicken."

---

## 12. Explainability

Whenever possible, Qavyo intelligence should explain why an insight exists.

For example:

"Labour cost increased because scheduled hours rose 18% while sales increased only 4%."

is more useful than:

"Labour efficiency decreased."

The system should preserve the calculations and evidence behind important outputs.

---

## 13. Automation Philosophy

Automation should remove repetitive operational work where doing so is safe and useful.

Examples might include:

- routine calculations
- reports
- anomaly detection
- reminders
- forecasting support
- suggested purchasing
- suggested staffing

High-impact actions should not be blindly automated without appropriate controls.

---

## 14. Multi-Location Potential

Although independent restaurants are important, knowledge should remain useful for restaurant groups where possible.

Potential multi-location concepts include:

- location comparisons
- cost differences
- supplier differences
- menu performance
- labour performance
- purchasing
- inventory
- operational benchmarks across locations

Do not force multi-location complexity into every feature.

## 15. Source Neutrality

Qavyo research may come from:

- restaurant operators
- restaurant consultants
- restaurant software companies
- accountants
- chefs
- hospitality experts
- industry reports
- educational videos
- podcasts
- social media
- forums
- competitor content

The creator's company does not determine whether the knowledge is useful.

Extract useful restaurant knowledge while separating it from promotional claims.

---

## 16. Competitor Content

Competitor educational content can be used as research.

However:

Do not treat competitor product behaviour as the desired Qavyo design automatically.

Separate:

restaurant problem

from:

competitor's particular solution.

Qavyo may solve the same underlying problem differently.

---

## 17. Qavyo Research Objective

The ultimate purpose of this repository is to build a high-quality restaurant operations intelligence library.

Over time, the library should make it possible to ask questions such as:

- What important restaurant KPIs should Qavyo calculate?
- What calculations can be automated using Qavyo data?
- What operational problems can Qavyo detect?
- What relationships exist between POS, inventory and labour?
- What signals predict margin problems?
- Which restaurant decisions are currently manual?
- What alerts could prevent losses?
- What information should restaurant managers see daily?
- What should Qavyo AI investigate automatically?
- Which feature opportunities appear repeatedly across independent sources?
- Which claims are supported by multiple sources?
- Which benchmarks vary by restaurant type or geography?

The repository should support evidence-based product development rather than feature brainstorming without evidence.
