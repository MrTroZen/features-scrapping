# Weekly Restaurant Dashboard and Variance Control

## Summary

The operational weekly financial and performance dashboard created by Inga Stanfield (Matanga's Pizzeria, 7 units, $7M+ revenue, National Pizzeria of the Year). The system tracks eight controllable metrics, provides exact forward-looking budget forecasting formulas for labor and food purchases, establishes strict threshold standards, and details the financial compounding of minor percentage slippages.

---

## 1. The Eight Controllable Metrics

| Metric | Target Standard | Measurement Source / Tracking Cadence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Weekly Sales Growth** | $\ge 10\%$ YoY Growth | POS MTD vs. Prior Year MTD / Weekly Forecast |
| **2. Labor Budget** | $19\%–20\%$ of Sales | POS Scheduled vs. Actual Labor Cost Dollars |
| **3. Food Purchase Budget** | $29\%$ of Sales | Total Vendor Invoices / Weekly Budget Cap |
| **4. Cash Deposit Variance** | $\$0.00$ ($\le \$3.00$ tolerance) | POS Cash Sales vs. Verified Bank Deposit |

| **7. Compliance Audit Score** | $\ge 95\%$ Passing Score | Weekly Store Cleanliness & Food Safety Inspection |
| **8. Training & Advancement** | $100\%$ Video / $\ge 95\%$ Quizzes | Learning Management System / Station Test Logs |

## 2. Budget Forecasting Formulas

### Forward-Looking Sales Projection
Budgets must be set proactively before schedules are posted and vendor orders placed, not analyzed reactively after month-end.

$$\text{Month-to-Date (MTD) Growth Rate} = \left(\frac{\text{Current Year MTD Sales}}{\text{Prior Year MTD Sales}}\right) - 1$$

$$\text{Projected Next Week Sales} = \text{Prior Year Same-Week Sales} \times (1 + \text{MTD Growth Rate})$$

*Operational Example*:
- Prior Year MTD = $\$80,000$; Current Year MTD = $\$92,000$.
- Growth Rate: $(\$92,000 / \$80,000) - 1 = +15\%$.
- Prior Year Same-Week Sales = $\$10,000$.
- **Projected Sales Next Week**: $\$10,000 \times (1 + 0.15) = \$11,500$.

### Labor Dollar Budgeting
$$\text{Dollar Labor Budget} = \text{Projected Next Week Sales} \times \text{Labor Target \%}$$

*Operational Example*:
$$\$11,500 \times 20\% = \$2,300\text{ Max Labor Spend}$$
The store general manager is restricted to scheduling and spending exactly $\$2,300$ across all store shifts for the upcoming week.

### Food Purchase Dollar Cap
$$\text{Dollar Purchase Budget} = \text{Projected Next Week Sales} \times \text{Purchase Target \%}$$

*Operational Example*:
$$\$11,500 \times 29\% = \$3,335\text{ Max Vendor Spend}$$
The kitchen manager or GM is restricted to spending a combined $\$3,335$ across all distributors (broadline, produce, dairy, beverage) for that week. Any order exceeding this figure requires written owner approval before purchase order submission.

## 3. Operational Standards & Variance Thresholds

### Cash Reconciliation & Theft Prevention
$$\text{Required Bank Deposit} = \text{Daily POS Cash Sales} - \text{Drawer Float Balance (\$200.00)}$$
- **Standard**: Expected deposit variance must be $\$0.00$. A maximum tolerance of $\$3.00$ accounts for minor coin change errors.
- **The Compounding Cost of Cash Shortage**:
  $$\$20/\text{day shortage} = \$600/\text{month} = \$7,300/\text{year in direct bottom-line leakage per store}$$
- **Protocol**: The closing shift leader and the bank deposit handler must sign and log the exact deposit slip nightly.

### Kitchen Speed of Service: The 10-20-30 Rule
Measured via KDS (Kitchen Display System) load time from ticket receipt to bump:
- **Dine-In & Counter Carryout**: $\le 10\text{ minutes}$
- **Phone & Scheduled Digital Orders**: $\le 20\text{ minutes}$
- **Delivery Orders (Out the Door)**: $\le 30\text{ minutes}$

### Compliance & Advancement Gates
- **Compliance Audit**: Stores undergo weekly unannounced audits on food date labeling, refrigeration temperatures, and sanitation. Anything $<95\%$ is an immediate failure requiring same-day remediation.
- **Training Progression**: New hires must maintain $100\%$ video SOP completion and score $\ge 95\%$ on station skill tests before working peak shifts unassisted.

## 4. The Compounding Math of Operational Slippage

> *"A small percentage of a big number is a big number."*

Operators frequently dismiss a 1%–2% variance in prime cost as trivial noise. In multi-unit or high-volume dining, this destroys owner distributions:

$$\text{Weekly Sales per Unit} = \$20,000$$
$$\text{2\% Labor Slippage} = \$400/\text{week}$$
$$\text{Annual Single-Store Loss} = \$400 \times 52 = \$20,800$$
$$\text{Annual Multi-Unit Loss (7 Locations)} = \$20,800 \times 7 = \$145,600\text{ in lost annual net profit}$$

## 5. Management Accountability Architecture

1. **Strict Tuesday Rhythm**: Weekly executive dashboard review takes place every Tuesday at 10:00 AM. Managers report variances before the week closes.
2. **Single Metric Ownership**: Every KPI has exactly one owner (e.g., GM owns labor; BOH Lead owns food purchases). If two managers share a metric, neither takes responsibility.
3. **Denominate Feedback in Cash, Not Percentages**: Supervisors must translate percentages into actual bank currency: *"We overspent $400 in cash last week, which is $20,800 a year gone."*
4. **Disciplinary Thresholds**: Documented 3-strike policy:
   - Week 1–2 Off-target: Corrective coaching and action plan.
   - Continued Unapproved Variance: Written warning.
   - Three Written Warnings: Demotion from management or termination.
