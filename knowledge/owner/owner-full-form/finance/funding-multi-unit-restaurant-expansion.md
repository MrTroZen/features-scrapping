# Key Information

- Compares four primary financing and structural pathways for expanding independent restaurants from a single unit to multi-unit scale:
  1. **Franchising:** Operator licenses the brand and systems in exchange for an upfront franchise fee ($30k–$50k) and ongoing royalties (typically 4% to 6% of gross sales). While capital-light, modern multi-unit winners (Chipotle, CAVA, Sweetgreen) avoid it due to steep FDD legal setup costs, franchisee friction, brand dilution, and losing 95% of unit operating profit.
  2. **Private Equity / Growth Equity:** Institutional equity investment used to fund rapid corporate store rollouts. Demands verified, industry-leading unit economics (20% to 25% store-level EBITDA, sub-3-year payback, >$2M AUV). Unlocks massive scale, but requires surrendering equity control and answering to institutional boards.
  3. **Organic Cash-Flow Reinvestment ("The Snowball Model"):** Retaining 100% of unit profits to self-fund subsequent locations. Progress is deliberate between Unit 1 and Unit 3, but compounds once a cluster of 3 to 4 profitable stores generates sufficient combined free cash flow to open 1 to 2 new units annually without debt or outside dilution.
  4. **SBA 7(a) & Commercial Debt Financing:** Government-guaranteed bank financing covering up to 80% to 90% of expansion CapEx. Enables operators to retain 100% equity and leverage operational cash flow to amortize debt, but requires strong personal credit (680+), collateral, and personal guarantees.

# Numbers / Calculations

- Franchise royalty take: Franchisors typically capture only 4% to 6% of top-line revenue, leaving 94% to 96% with the franchisee; corporate-owned operators retain 100% of store-level net profit (15% to 25% net margins on strong concepts).
- FDD legal barrier: Establishing a legal Franchise Disclosure Document and state registration costs $50,000 to $100,000+ before selling a single unit.
- SBA loan leverage: SBA 7(a) loans typically require only 10% to 20% equity down payment against verified historical cash flow of the flagship location.
- Multi-unit cash compounding: A cluster of 4 units averaging $200,000 annual net cash flow each produces $800,000 in annual internal expansion capital, sufficient to build out 1 to 2 second-generation units every year self-funded.

# Useful Method / Takeaway

- **The 3-Unit Self-Funded Rule:** Build Units 1, 2, and 3 through retained earnings or SBA debt before entertaining outside private equity; institutional valuations and deal terms improve exponentially once multi-unit management is proven.
- **Franchising Rejection for Quality Concepts:** If culinary execution, hospitality, and brand reputation are central to the concept's defensibility, avoid franchising; corporate ownership preserves operational control.
- **Debt vs Equity Cost of Capital:** When interest rates are manageable, bank debt (SBA) is far cheaper than equity dilution over the long term because equity ownership is permanent whereas loans get paid off.
