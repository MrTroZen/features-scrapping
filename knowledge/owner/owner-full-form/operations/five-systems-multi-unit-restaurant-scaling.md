# Five Operational Systems for Multi-Unit Restaurant Scaling

## Summary

The operational operating system codified by Inga Stanfield (Matanga's Pizzeria, 7 units, $7M+ revenue, >9% net margin, 2025 National Pizzeria of the Year). The framework details five non-negotiable management architectures that decouple restaurant scaling from founder burnout: the weekly Money Map rhythm, the Gradual Release of Responsibility (GRR) delegation framework, station-level time-engineered operations, the 12-month automated marketing engine, and the standardized guest service recovery matrix.

---

## 1. System 1: The Money Map (Weekly Financial Rhythm)

Operators checking P&Ls monthly or bi-annually suffer from lag-time blindness, allowing minor weekly leaks ($1,000/week) to compound into $24,000+ budget deficits before detection.

### Operational Structure
- **Standing Cadence**: Every Friday at 7:30 AM via video conference with the head bookkeeper.
- **Full-Burdened Labor Review**: Review total gross wages including FICA, FUTA, SUTA, employer taxes, worker's compensation, and overtime.
- **Forward Cash-Flow Modeling**: Review upcoming capital expenditures, recurring equipment repairs (e.g., $5,000 deck oven servicing), annual licensing/permit fees, and unpaid corporate catering receivables.
- **Automated Bank Alerts**: Program automated notifications directly from commercial banking accounts that trigger if vendor ACH debits, payroll draws, or operational withdrawals exceed pre-set weekly budget caps.

## 2. System 2: The GRR Delegation Framework (Gradual Release of Responsibility)

To eliminate the "founder retraining loop" where owners spend 20 hours/week repeating verbal instructions to high-turnover entry-level staff, operational knowledge must be permanently captured in video SOPs and transferred through the 4-stage GRR method:

```
[Stage 1: I Do, You Watch] ──> [Stage 2: I Do, You Help] ──> [Stage 3: You Do, I Help] ──> [Stage 4: You Do, I Cheer]
```

### Four Stages of Operational Delegation

| Stage | Execution Role | Supervisor Role | Debrief & Verification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. I Do, You Watch** | Owner/General Manager | Trainee observes | Review why specific questions/steps exist; explain scoring rubric. |
| **2. I Do, You Help** | Owner/General Manager | Trainee handles sub-tasks | Provide immediate feedback on trainee delivery; solicit trainee candidate evaluation. |
| **3. You Do, I Help** | Trainee leads primary flow | Owner/GM observes | Intervene only for clarifying points; evaluate trainee's independent judgment. |
| **4. You Do, I Cheer** | Trainee owns end-to-end | Owner/GM affirms | Full autonomous delegation confirmed; manager executes unassisted. |

## 3. System 3: Station-Level Operations and Time Engineering

A well-run store requires that every line station is standardized such that two different line cooks produce identical items in identical timeframes.

### Operational Protocols:
- **Visual Recipe Bibles**: Photo-heavy recipe sheets detailing exact gram/ounce portions, assembly order, and final plating standards.
- **Time-Engineered Station Closings**:
  - Closing station tasks must be sequenced and timed with a stopwatch.
  - *Standard*: Make-line disassembly, pan breakdown, and sanitization ("gutting the make-line") must be executed in exactly **15 to 18 minutes**.
- **Service Cadence Standards**: Documented thresholds for customer greetings ($<30\text{ seconds}$), table check-ins, and ticket fire-to-table delivery windows.

## 4. System 4: The 12-Month Growth Engine

Eliminates the "feast-or-famine" marketing cycle where owners market only when sales drop, get overwhelmed during rushes, abandon marketing, and experience another sales collapse 4 to 6 weeks later.

### Five-Step Campaign Architecture:
1. **Core Story & Theme**: Define the annual hook 12 months in advance (e.g., Pi Day, Valentine's, Halloween, Local Sports Season).
2. **Quantified Asset Production**: Standardized creative requirement per campaign:
   $$\text{Campaign Creative Pack} = 2\text{ Videos} + 4\text{ High-Res Photos} + 3\text{ Ad/Copy Captions}$$
3. **Multi-Touchpoint Distribution Matrix**: Coordinated release across 7 touchpoints:
   - In-store register counter signs and table tents.
   - Receipt footer promotional codes.
   - Website hero banner and online ordering pop-ups.
   - Pizza box toppers / flyers.
   - Automated email broadcasts and SMS push alerts.
4. **Ad Fuel Budget Benchmark**: Paid social/search advertising is capped at strictly **1% of gross revenue** (significantly below enterprise chain averages of 3%–5%).
5. **Batch Scheduling**: Marketing campaigns are planned quarterly, batch-photographed, and pre-scheduled in automation tools to run uninterrupted during operational surges.

## 5. System 5: Standardized Service Recovery Matrix

Eliminates emotional founder distress and inconsistent frontline responses by defining a clear lookup table for customer friction points.

### Recovery Matrix Lookup Table

| Operational Failure | Standard Immediate Remedy | Long-Term Prevention Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delivery Time $>90$ Minutes** | 100% Full Order Refund | Audit driver dispatch logs; adjust online ordering lead times in POS. |
| **Missing Topping or Side Item** | Immediate $\$5.00$ partial refund | Retrain expeditor; verify box check-off system at packing table. |
| **Incorrect Pizza / Wrong Item** | Immediate priority remake + free redelivery + next-order VIP coupon | Log cook error in station shift tracking log. |
| **Cold Food Upon Arrival** | Immediate replacement or store credit | Inspect hot bags and driver departure intervals. |

### Frontline Execution Rule:
Frontline supervisors are trained using standard scripts and authorized to issue immediate refunds/replacements within the matrix limits without calling the owner or general manager.
