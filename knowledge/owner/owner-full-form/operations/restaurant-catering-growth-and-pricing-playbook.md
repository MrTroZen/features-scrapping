# Restaurant Catering Growth, B2B Acquisition, and Pricing Playbook

## Summary

An operational blueprint by Inga Stanfield (Matanga's Pizzeria, 7 units, hundreds of thousands in annual catering revenue) detailing how to build a six-figure restaurant catering profit engine. The framework establishes the economics of migrating from third-party catering marketplaces (e.g., ezCater) to direct native ordering, ten catering-specific menu engineering rules, the "Sponsored Lunch" B2B corporate client acquisition machine, the "Yes and Pivot" fulfillment rule, and exact mathematical formulas for pricing catering orders and calculating labor burdens.

---

## 1. Direct Native Catering Economics vs. Marketplaces

Third-party corporate catering marketplaces (e.g., ezCater) charge commission fees ranging between $20\%\text{ and }30\%$.

### Margin Recovery Math
Migrating $\$10,000/\text{month}$ in corporate catering from third-party marketplaces to native direct ordering recaptures:

$$\$10,000 \times 20\%–30\% = \$2,000–\$3,000/\text{month}\ (\$24,000–\$36,000/\text{year})\text{ straight to net profit}$$

### Long-Term Asset Value
Third-party platforms mask customer contact information. Direct ordering captures corporate administrative emails and phone numbers, enabling automated monthly re-marketing to executive assistants and office managers at zero Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).

## 2. Ten Rules for Catering Menu Engineering

Catering operations require fundamentally different recipes, packaging, and pricing structures than dining room service:

1. **Price Exclusively on a Per-Person Basis**: Never accept lump-sum budgets upfront (e.g., a customer requesting to feed 150 people for $\$300$ is requesting $\$2.00/\text{guest}$). Framing packages per person immediately anchors realistic expectations.
2. **Strategic Bundle Pricing**: Display tray pricing, guest yield, and per-person cost breakdown:
   - *Half-tray pasta & meatballs*: $\$45.00$ (serves 12) = $\$3.75/\text{person}$
   - *Caesar salad bowl*: $\$35.00$ (serves 10) = $\$3.50/\text{person}$
   - *Breadstick tray*: $\$20.00$ (serves 10) = $\$2.00/\text{person}$
   - *Bundled Package*: *"Complete Italian Buffet for $9.25 per person."*
3. **Dedicated Catering SKUs**: Offer bulk-production items that make economic sense only at scale (e.g., multi-layer sheet lasagna, frozen-to-defrost sheet brownies) that are never offered on daily dine-in menus.
4. **Packaging as Plating**: Food presentation inside travel containers represents the restaurant's brand standard. Salads must be packed with dressing packets on the side to prevent wilting during transit.
5. **Pre-Stock Disposables**: Maintain designated catering shelves stocked with chafing wire racks, sternos, plastic serving tongs, heavy-duty cutlery kits, and branded napkins before bookings occur.
6. **Dietary Accommodation Bundles**: Corporate orders almost universally require vegetarian, gluten-free, or high-protein options; menus offering explicit dietary tags win corporate catering bids.
7. **Instant Digital Ordering vs. Contact Forms**: While complex galas or weddings require consultation forms, weekday corporate office lunches (20–40 guests) require frictionless instant checkout without email delays.
8. **Kitchen Ticket Component Clarity**: POS kitchen tickets for catering must specify precise component quantities (e.g., *"Half Tray Garlic Bread — exactly 24 units"*), preventing prep cooks from guessing.
9. **Variety Reduction Advisory**: Advise clients against excessive menu options (e.g., choosing 4 different pastas and 3 salads). When buffets offer excessive variety, guests take portions of everything, which slows line service, increases overall client cost, and complicates kitchen assembly.
10. **Re-Order Loyalty Incentives**: Include physical flyers offering high-perceived-value product bonuses (e.g., a free half-tray of breadsticks or a gallon of sweet tea) on their first direct reorder.

## 3. B2B Corporate Client Acquisition Machine

Corporate offices order repeatedly throughout the year with average ticket sizes ranging from $\$400\text{ to }\$1,500+$.

```
[Target: 20+ Employee Offices] ──> [Sponsored Lunch Hook ($15 COGS)] ──> [Admin Loyalty Gift] ──> [Recurring Corporate Reorders]
```

### Strategy 1: The "Sponsored Lunch" Hook
- **Targeting**: Identify businesses with $\ge 20$ on-site employees within a 3- to 5-mile radius (banks, medical clinics, dental groups, car dealerships, tech offices).
- **The Pitch**: Dedicated catering outreach staff calls/emails the office manager:
  > *"We are selecting three local community businesses this week to sponsor a complimentary lunch for up to 12 team members. Our only request is that someone picks it up and snaps a quick team photo for our social media."*
- **The Financial Equation**:
  - Three large pizzas cost the restaurant $\$12.00–\$15.00$ in wholesale food cost.
  - Pickup requirement eliminates delivery labor and vehicle expense.
  - **The Immediate Upsell**: In $\approx 33\%$ of cases, the office coordinator responds: *"We actually have 24 people in our office. Can we pay for the additional pizzas?"* The paid upsell immediately offsets the food cost of the entire promotion.
  - **Lifetime Value Case Study**: A single San Antonio energy company engaged via sponsored lunch ordered four subsequent corporate events totaling over **$10,000 in gross catering revenue**.

### Strategy 2: Off-Peak Administrative Drop-Offs
During slow mid-afternoon hours (2:00 PM – 4:00 PM), managers or drivers drop off fresh shareable dessert samples (donuts, cookies) and physical catering menus with QR codes directly to office receptionists.

### Strategy 3: Rewarding the Corporate Point of Contact
Corporate coordinators receive zero financial benefit from company funds spent on catering. Whenever an administrative assistant or event coordinator completes an order, present them personally with a **complimentary 16-inch family pizza gift certificate** as personal hospitality.

## 4. Operational Execution and the "Yes and Pivot" Method

- **Zero-Tolerance for Tardiness**: A late delivery publically embarrasses the corporate organizer in front of company leadership and permanently terminates the relationship.
- **The "Yes and Pivot" Technique for Large Last-Minute Orders**:
  - Rather than turning away last-minute requests (e.g., feeding 120 people on 2 hours' notice), accept the business and negotiate operational variables:
  - *Recipe Pivot*: *"We cannot bake five trays of lasagna in 2 hours, but we can have five hot trays of spaghetti and meatballs ready for you."*
  - *Staggered Delivery Pivot*: *"We can deliver the first 15 pizzas at 11:30 AM and the remaining 15 pizzas at 12:15 PM."*

## 5. Mathematical Formulas: Pricing and Labor Allocation

### 1. Catering Price Formula
To achieve a target net profit margin while covering all variable inputs:

$$\text{Catering Price} = \frac{\text{Total Variable Costs}}{1 - \text{Target Net Profit Margin}}$$

#### Worked Operational Example (85 Guests):
- Wholesale Food Cost: $\$360.00$
- Direct Labor Cost: $\$250.00$
- Packaging & Chafing Supplies: $\$50.00$
- Vehicle Delivery Expense: $\$50.00$
- **Total Combined Costs**: $\$710.00$
- **Target Net Profit Margin**: $15\%\ (0.15)$

$$\text{Price} = \frac{\$710.00}{1 - 0.15} = \frac{\$710.00}{0.85} = \$835.29$$

$$\text{Per-Person Price} = \frac{\$835.29}{85\text{ Guests}} = \$9.82\text{ per guest (plus applicable taxes)}$$

---

### 2. Full-Service Catering Labor Calculation
For off-site events requiring full staffing, labor is budgeted across six operational phases:
1. Prep & staging
2. Line cooking
3. Transportation & delivery
4. Chafing & buffet setup
5. On-site guest service
6. Breakdown & dish breakdown

$$\text{Total Direct Wages} = \text{Total Scheduled Labor Hours} \times \text{Average Hourly Wage}$$
$$\text{Full Labor Cost} = \text{Total Direct Wages} \times (1 + \text{Payroll Tax / Burden Rate})$$

#### Worked Example:
- Required Labor Hours: $53\text{ hours}$
- Average Hourly Wage: $\$18.00/\text{hr}$
- Payroll Tax / Insurance Burden: $15\%\ (1.15)$

$$\text{Direct Wages} = 53 \times \$18.00 = \$954.00$$
$$\text{Full Labor Burden} = \$954.00 \times 1.15 = \$1,097.10$$

### The Catering Labor Benchmark Rule:
$$\text{Target Catering Labor \%} \le 20\% \text{ of Gross Catering Revenue}$$
If calculated labor exceeds $20\%$, the operator is undercharging for event labor or over-allocating on-site staffing.
