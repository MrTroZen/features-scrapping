# Four Non-Negotiable Restaurant Operating Rules

## Summary

An operational philosophy codified by Inga Stanfield (Matanga's Pizzeria, 7 units, 2025 National Pizzeria of the Year) establishing timeless management principles that transcend macroeconomic cycles. The framework details early-stage customer acquisition ("Sales Before People" and the 5-stops-a-day method), dual proactive and reactive labor/food cost controls, operational root-cause remediation ("Find It, Fix It, and Never See It Again" applied to order accuracy), and founder developmental stages.

---

## 1. Rule 1: Sales Before People (Early-Stage Mandate)

During the first 24 months of a restaurant's lifecycle, achieving operational break-even and sustainable profitability is the primary leadership duty. An unprofitable restaurant cannot provide wage stability, benefits, or career pathways for staff.

### The Five-Stops-a-Day Technique (Grassroots Local Acquisition)
Before investing heavily in digital advertising, operators must establish deep physical community ties within a 3-mile catchment area:
- **Routine**: The founder visits five local commercial or civic establishments daily (schools, churches, medical practices, retail storefronts).
- **The Touchpoint**: Provide complimentary food samples with a brief personal greeting: *"We are the independent pizzeria down the street; we wanted to bring your team lunch and introduce ourselves."*
- **The Math of Local Compounding**:
  $$\text{5 stops/day} \times \text{5 days/week} = 25\text{ establishments/week}$$
  $$25\text{ locations} \times 10\text{ employees/location} = 250\text{ direct local contacts/week}$$
  $$250 \times 4\text{ weeks} = 1,000\text{ local residents personally sampled every month}$$
- **Civic Event Floats**: Participating in municipal holiday parades (Memorial Day, 4th of July, Christmas) with a branded utility vehicle involves minimal entry fees ($\$50–\$100$) and exposes the brand directly to thousands of neighborhood families in hours.

## 2. Rule 2: The Math Does Not Lie (Proactive vs. Reactive Controls)

### Pizzeria Financial Benchmark Architecture
- **Occupancy / Base Rent**: $7\%–8\%$ of gross sales.
- **Cost of Goods Sold (Food Cost)**: $\le 30\%$ of gross sales.
- **Direct Hourly Line Labor**: $23\%–25\%$ of gross sales (excluding payroll taxes, FUTA, and SUTA).

### Proactive Flight Planning vs. In-Flight Reactive Corrections
Managing prime cost functions like piloting an aircraft: the pre-shift budget represents the flight plan, while shift-level management represents mid-air adjustments.

```
[Proactive: MTD Trend Forecast] ──> [Pre-Published Schedule Dollar Cap] ──> [Pre-Approved Purchase Cap]
                                                 │
                                                 ▼
[Reactive: Mid-Shift Corrections] ──> [POS Hour-to-Date Tracking] ──> ["First to Go" Protocol]
```

### Eliminating the "Weekend Subsidization" Error
- **The Flawed Approach**: Running $30\%–32\%$ labor Monday through Wednesday and forcing labor down to $17\%$ Friday through Sunday to average $23\%$ for the week.
- **The Operational Consequence**: Starving the kitchen of labor during peak weekend volume degrades ticket times, compromises cleanliness, and burns out top performers.
- **The Standard**: Trim hours aggressively on low-volume weekday shifts (scheduling single-cook coverage and cross-trained cashier/expeditors) to preserve adequate labor hours for peak Friday–Sunday prep and execution.

### The "First to Go" Mid-Shift Protocol
When shift sales track below projections, management must cut hours without creating staff resentment:
- **Protocol**: Team members who clocked in earliest are the first offered or assigned to clock out (e.g., a 9:00 AM prep cook leaves at 1:30 PM rather than an 11:30 AM arrival).
- **Objective Clarity**: Establishes transparent fairness, removing perceptions of management favoritism.

## 3. Rule 3: Find It, Fix It, and Never See It Again

When recurring errors arise (e.g., missing $0.75–$0.99 dipping cups causing guest dissatisfaction and 1-star reviews), operators must resist blaming frontline staff and instead audit the 3-step operational breakdown:

```
[1. Set Precise Standard] ──> [2. Train via GRR Method] ──> [3. Daily Closed-Loop Enforcement]
```

### The Box-Check Verification Standard
1. **The Standard**: Every takeout and delivery box must be opened at the counter, visually matched item-by-item against the printed kitchen ticket, and verified out loud before handoff to the guest or delivery courier.
2. **Training Cadence**: Train the protocol during slow mid-afternoon shifts using the Gradual Release of Responsibility (GRR) framework to build muscle memory before Friday rush periods.
3. **Enforcement & Disciplinary Triggers**:
   - Store GMs review all customer reviews and internal feedback logs weekly.
   - If an order accuracy issue is reported, the shift captain receives a documented coaching slip requiring frontline retraining.
   - Three consecutive weeks of the same operational failure initiates formal disciplinary demotion or termination.

## 4. Rule 4: The Founder Evolution Curve

The operational capabilities required to open a single location will fail at scale. Founders must intentionally transition across three distinct evolutionary phases:

```
Phase 1: Sales & Hustle (0-2 Yrs) ──> Phase 2: Systems & SOPs (2-4 Yrs) ──> Phase 3: Leadership Development (Scaling)
```

1. **Phase 1: Sales & Hustle (Years 0–2)**: Focus is survival, grassroots community acquisition, and achieving positive operating cash flow.
2. **Phase 2: Operational Codification (Years 2–4)**: Focus shifts to eliminating single points of failure by documenting station timing standards, recipe bibles, and inventory par systems.
3. **Phase 3: Leadership Factory & Enterprise Governance (Years 4+)**: Focus shifts to developing autonomous general managers, multi-unit scorecards, and capital allocation.
