# Key Information

- Comprehensive operational guide on launching or expanding a restaurant for under $10,000 using distressed asset acquisitions (second-generation takeovers) rather than conventional six-figure ground-up buildouts:
- **Distressed Asset Purchase vs Business Purchase:** In an asset buy, the operator purchases only the physical infrastructure (hoods, grease trap, walk-ins, dining furniture) and assumes the lease of a shuttered or failing concept, rather than buying the previous operator's entity, debt, or payroll obligations.
- **The Seller Leverage Dynamic:** Shuttered restaurant operators who signed personal lease guarantees face ongoing monthly rent liabilities ($3,000 to $10,000/month) with zero incoming revenue. They are motivated to sell $200,000+ in physical improvements for $5,000 to $10,000 (or seller financing) simply to secure a lease release from the landlord.
- **Sourcing Distressed Opportunities:** Filtering platforms like BizBuySell specifically for "Asset Sales" under the restaurant category, or physically scouting strip centers for newly closed concepts before listings become public.
- **Crucial Corporate Structuring:** Always execute asset purchases through a clean, newly formed LLC with a specialized asset purchase agreement (APA). Never purchase the prior owner's corporate entity to prevent inheriting payroll tax liens, vendor debts, or slip-and-fall liabilities.
- **Lease Gotchas & Pitfalls to Eliminate:**
  - *Kickout Clauses:* Landlord rights to terminate the lease early based on arbitrary sales thresholds.
  - *Uncapped CAM (Common Area Maintenance):* Open-ended pass-through expenses for parking lot or exterior repairs; negotiate hard percentage caps or flat fees.
  - *Term Length:* Ensure a minimum of 3 to 5 years remaining on the active term plus pre-negotiated 5-year renewal options.

# Numbers / Calculations

- Startup CapEx: Conventional independent restaurant buildouts average $250,000 to $600,000+; distressed asset takeovers frequently close between $5,000 and $10,000 in upfront purchase cash.
- Case Study (Doo-Dah Diner, Wichita, KS): Purchased closed Chinese restaurant assets for $7,500; scaled the resulting diner concept to over $10 Million in cumulative sales across 12 years.
- Physical infrastructure savings:
  - Type 1 commercial hood installation: ~$30,000 to $60,000 avoided.
  - In-ground grease trap installation: ~$15,000 to $30,000 avoided.
  - Walk-in cooler/freezer complex: ~$20,000 to $40,000 avoided.
  - Total mechanical/plumbing savings: Often exceeds $150,000 in sunk infrastructure.

# Useful Method / Takeaway

- **Ignore Online Asking Prices:** Published asking prices on business broker sites ($50,000+) are aspirational; when an operator is hemorrhaging rent on a dark storefront, cash offers between $5,000 and $10,000 with clean lease assumption terms are routinely accepted.
- **Evaluate Location Failure vs Concept Failure:** Audit why the prior tenant closed. If failure stemmed from poor food, uninspired marketing, or absentee management, the site is viable. If failure was caused by zero vehicular ingress/egress or hidden crime, reject the site.
- **Landlord Lease Assignment Leverage:** Present the landlord with a polished business plan, credit history, and menu concept as a solution to their dark vacancy, using that leverage to renegotiate pre-pandemic per-square-foot rates downward.
