# Key Information

- How you read your restaurant's P&L. Sales, cost of goods sold, labor costs, and other costs are the four parts. The total sales is the total amount of income that you made this month. Part two is your cost of goods sold. Your COGS need to be broken down into the exact same categories that your sales is broken out in. The third part of your P&L is the labor breakdown by wages, taxes, benefits, and insurance. Part two, food cost/cost of goods sold, and part three, labor costs, together make up what's called your prime costs. Then part four of your P&L is your operating expenses. Some of those are firmly fixed, like your rent. Other operating expenses might actually change depending on your sales. You don't sell credit card fees, but the busier you are, the higher those get. The next piece of your P&L is your profit, which is your sales minus your cost of goods sold minus your labor costs minus your operating expenses. This is usually labeled net income on your P&L. And that is your real profit.
