# Key Information

- Restaurant owners calculate pour cost but frequently forget operational overhead.
- Garnishes, napkins, POS merchant fees, glassware breakage, and bartender labor all add up significantly.
- An $8.50 Tom Collins that looks like a 33% pour cost drink on paper is often closer to 40% real cost once operational overhead is factored.

# Numbers / Calculations

- An $8.50 Tom Collins that looks like a 33% pour cost drink on paper is often closer to 40% real cost once operational overhead is factored.
- Raising a signature cocktail from $8.50 to $10 recovers thousands in profit across annual volume from a single menu item.

# Useful Method / Takeaway

- Operators should price drinks based on total contribution margin and labor intensity rather than simple raw ingredient cost.
